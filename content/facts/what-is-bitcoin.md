Bitcoin is peer-to-peer electronic cash — digital money that moves between
people or computers with no trusted middleman (like a bank) in between, and
whose issuance isn't under any single party's control.

Before Bitcoin, digital payments always needed a middleman. Physical
cash works differently: when you hand someone a $20 bill, they just
check it with their own eyes and fingers — no bank required. But as
payments went digital, we lost that: a credit card network, PayPal, or
WeChat now has to approve and verify every payment, because digital
bits (unlike physical cash) need someone trusted to control their
transfer.

**What Bitcoin fixes here:** Digital payments had always needed
somebody in the middle to approve them. Bitcoin brings the "just check
it yourself" property of cash back to money that's made of nothing but
bits.

**Analogy:** Like emailing someone a photo — except that the moment it
arrives, your copy is gone. Everything else you send digitally is
really a copy; this is the first kind that actually leaves your hands.
