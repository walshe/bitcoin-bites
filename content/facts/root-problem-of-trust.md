When Satoshi announced Bitcoin, the very first thing they explained wasn't
the technology — it was the problem. In their own words: "The root problem
with conventional currency is all the trust that's required to make it
work." Central banks must be trusted not to debase the currency. Banks must
be trusted to hold your money and not lend it all out. You have to trust
them with your privacy, and trust they won't let identity thieves drain
your account.

Before Bitcoin, every layer of the financial system was really just a
chain of promises — from your bank, to the clearing houses, to the central
bank — and the whole system only worked as long as every link in that chain
kept its word.

**Analogy:** Handing someone cash requires trusting nothing about them.
Routing a payment through a bank means trusting an entire chain of people
you've never met, and never will.
