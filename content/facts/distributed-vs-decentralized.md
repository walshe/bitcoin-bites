These two words get used interchangeably, but they describe different
things. A system is **distributed** simply because its data lives in more
than one place — copies of the ledger sit on many different computers
instead of one. A system is **decentralized** because no single party is
in charge of it — no company or person can unilaterally change the
rules or rewrite history.

A system can be distributed without being decentralized: a company
running the same database on ten of its own servers is still fully in
control. That distinction matters a lot once you're judging whether a
network actually removes a trusted middleman, or just spreads that
middleman's infrastructure around.

**Analogy:** Ten security cameras filming the same store, all owned by
the store, are distributed — but the store still decides what happens
to the footage. That's distributed without being decentralized.
