Bitcoin wasn't the first attempt at digital cash. DigiCash, created by
David Chaum in 1989, tried something similar — but it depended on one
company's central server to issue money and verify every payment. When
that company ran into trouble, so did the money built on top of it.

Centrally controlled private money keeps failing for the same reason:
people can't rely on money that vanishes when the issuing company goes
bankrupt, gets hacked, or gets shut down by a government.

**What Bitcoin fixes here:** There's no single server or company to
depend on in the first place — the network is the "company," spread
across thousands of independent computers with no owner, so there's
nothing for a bankruptcy or a court order to switch off.

**Analogy:** The difference between a single food truck (gone overnight if
the owner quits) and an entire city's worth of independently-run
restaurants (no single owner to shut them all down at once).
