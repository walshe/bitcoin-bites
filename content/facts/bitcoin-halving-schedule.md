The coinbase reward a miner earns for winning a block isn't fixed
forever — it's hardcoded to cut in half roughly every four years: 50
bitcoin per block at the very start, then 25, then 12.5, and so on,
each halving slowing down how fast new bitcoin enters circulation.
Follow that curve far enough and it explains the 21 million cap:
issuance keeps shrinking toward (but never quite reaching) zero, with
the final sliver expected to be mined around the year 2140.

Nobody votes on when a halving happens or decides to delay it — it's
just a rule inside the same open-source software every participant
runs, triggered by block count rather than the calendar.

**Analogy:** Like a bag of rice that halves in size every scoop you
take out — cutting in half forever, but never actually running out.
