Nobody votes on Bitcoin's rules. Instead, four overlapping groups each
hold a check on the others, with no single group able to force a
change through: nodes choose which software (and which rules) to
run and enforce; miners spend real energy competing under whatever
rules those nodes have already accepted; users and investors decide
the coin's market value, and can simply dump a currency that changed
in a way they don't like; and developers can propose and peer-review
improvements, but can't make anyone actually install them.

Try to unilaterally double the 21 million coin cap, and it plays out
the same way regardless of which group attempts it: the other three
groups just don't go along, and the change is worthless. It's closer to
a free market held in equilibrium by self-interest than to a
government passing laws.

**Analogy:** Like four people each holding one key to the same safe —
no single key-holder, however senior, can open it alone; a change
only happens when enough of the others independently agree to turn
their key too.
