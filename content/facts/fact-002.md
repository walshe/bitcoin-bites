Before Bitcoin, "owning" money digitally always meant a bank or company kept a
ledger entry on your behalf — you never actually held it, you just trusted
their database (and their permission to let you spend it). Bitcoin is the
first form of digital money you can hold directly: a private key is a secret
number that lets you sign transactions, and whoever holds that key controls
the coins — no bank, company, or government needs to approve the transfer.

**Analogy:** A private key is less like a password (which a company can reset
or revoke) and more like the only key to a safe deposit box that nobody else,
not even the bank, has a copy of. Lose the key and the box is unreachable
forever; that's the trade-off for nobody else being able to open it either.
