Bitcoin's ledger itself can't really be censored — it only takes one
honest miner anywhere in the world to include your transaction, and
you could even mine it yourself. But Bitcoin still normally travels
over the ordinary internet, and a government determined to clamp
down could simply block that traffic at the border, the same way it
might block any other website or service.

Projects like the Blockstream Satellite route around that entirely: with
a small dish, anyone on Earth can receive a live broadcast of the
Bitcoin blockchain beamed down from space, no local internet service
provider required. Pair that with off-grid mesh-networking tools like
TxTenna, and it becomes extremely difficult for any single country to
cut its population off from Bitcoin, even by controlling every internet
connection crossing its borders.

**What Bitcoin fixes here:** Cutting a population off from Bitcoin
would mean controlling not just every internet cable crossing the
border, but the sky above it too.

**Analogy:** Like a radio station broadcasting from orbit — jamming
every road into a country doesn't help if the news is falling out of
the sky everywhere at once.
