A mining pool's published hash-rate share can look like one company
controls that much of the network, but a pool is really just thousands
of independent miners choosing to point their hardware at it — and
they can walk away just as easily as they joined. In 2014, the pool
Ghash.io crept up toward roughly half of Bitcoin's total hash rate.
Rather than wait to see what a near-majority pool might do, individual
miners started voluntarily leaving for smaller pools, since the coin
they were mining loses value for everyone (including them) if the
network looks centralized enough to be attacked.

Nobody had to force that outcome. It happened because every miner
in the pool had a direct financial stake in Bitcoin staying
decentralized enough to be trusted.

**Analogy:** Like shareholders quietly selling out of a company the
moment one shareholder starts accumulating a controlling stake — no
vote required, just everyone protecting what their shares are worth.
